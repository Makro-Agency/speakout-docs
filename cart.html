Cart and checkout

Where the store's rules show themselves: the fee explains itself, one plan per order, and the phone-check gate that keeps a not-ready order from checking out.

Open the live page ↗

What the customer sees

  • The plan line with its province and billing period under the title, and a Remove link.
  • The 911 fee line with its plain-language explainer ("set by your province... can't be removed. We show it here so there are no surprises."), and deliberately no remove link.
  • The SIM band: "Using your own SIM. No SIM added." with an Add a SIM link, or the SIM item if one is in the order.
  • Order summary: subtotal, the fee as its own row, taxes at checkout, total, and "You pay in full today".

The rules it enforces

SituationWhat the cart does
Second plan addedReplaces the first plan and its fee. One plan per order, always.
Plan removedTakes its 911 fee out with it.
Quantity raisedReset to one, with a note.
Own-SIM order, phone check never ran"IMEI check incomplete" on the plan line, a red notice, and Checkout disabled until the check runs (on the plan's page) or a SIM is added.
Phone check ran but couldn't be confirmedThe notice stays visible so nothing is hidden, and Checkout stays open.
Plan tied to an unsupported province"Quebec - Service unavailable" on the line, and checkout blocked until the province is updated.
Checkout pressed without an accountThe customer is routed through account creation first, so the later activation has an owner.

Edit it, step by step

Open the editor on the cart.
Wording you can change here: the empty-cart message and its "browse plans" link label, the note under the Checkout button ("You'll activate your service after checkout..."), and the SIM band wording. Click the cart section and the fields are in its panel.
The rule messages (the red notices, "IMEI check incomplete", the fee explainer) are translation strings shared with the rest of the store; ask Makro for wording changes there.
Why an order might look "stuck" in testing If Checkout is grey, read the red line above it: it always names the reason (IMEI, or province). Nine times out of ten the fix is opening the plan's page, entering the IMEI, and re-adding.
